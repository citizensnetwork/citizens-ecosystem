## What I found while writing them

1. **Follow already has its tables, and Events already have a bookmark.** `follows` (profile to profile, and a Contributor is a profile) and `place_follows` exist; an Event has both **Connect** (going) and **Consider** (a bookmark), two states of one `rsvps` row. The app folds "save" into Follow for Contributors and Places on purpose (`entity-card.jsx`). The app already has Follow buttons on the profile pages and in the entity card (`toggleFollow`, `togglePlaceFollow` in `store.jsx`), but **not on the Map v2 sheet**. So P2-04 is really **Save** plus **Follow on the sheet** plus **notifications to followers**, not a new follow system. Adding an `event_follows` table would duplicate the RSVP.
2. **Follow notifications are a sketch, not a migration.** They need a decision about preferences first (question 3). The shape: two new notification types (`followed_new_event`, `followed_new_news`), and an `AFTER INSERT` trigger on `events` and on `news_posts` that inserts one `notifications` row per follower, capped per post, skipped for a person who turned that preference off, and never for a blocked pair. It will be written as its own migration once question 3 is answered.
3. **A privacy gap that already exists, and that P2-08 depends on.** `follows` and `place_follows` have a `SELECT` policy of `true` for the public role, and the `anon` role has `SELECT` on both (checked with `has_table_privilege`, 2026-10-15). Anyone holding the public key that every browser carries can list who follows whom. Two consequences: (a) the "show a follower count only from 100" rule (P2-08, so a small ministry is never shown a low number) can be sidestepped by counting the rows directly; (b) in a Christian community platform, a list of which people follow which ministry is a sensitive thing to leave readable by anyone. **Not changed**: tightening it (own rows only, with the bucketed count served by a function) needs an audit of everything that reads these tables first. It is a separate decision (RESUME item S13), listed below as question 4.

## Questions for Stephen (one line each, my recommendation first)

1. **Save** (`p2-04-saved-items.sql`): the app already folds "save" into a public Follow for Contributors and Places, and gives Events a Consider bookmark. Do you want a second, **private** bookmark for a Contributor or a Place (never counted, never shown to the listing), for a believer who wants to remember a ministry without being listed as its follower? _Recommended: yes, but only because Follow is publicly readable (item 3); if the follows gap is fixed first, Follow alone may be enough._
2. **Co-hosting** (`p2-09-event-cohosts.sql`): the organiser invites, the other Contributor accepts, at most 3 co-hosts. Is 3 the right ceiling? _Recommended: 3._
3. **Follower notifications**: when a Contributor you follow posts an event or news, should you be notified by default, or only after you switch it on? _Recommended: on by default for a Contributor you chose to follow, with one switch in Settings._
4. **The follows privacy gap** (S13): audit and tighten who can read `follows` and `place_follows` (and serve follower counts only in buckets)? _Recommended: yes, before anything makes following more visible on the map._
5. **Moments** (`p2-05-moments.sql`): the tracker's own rule (section 4, **Q4 Attention**: "does it respect people's time? Reject or defer if it relies on streaks, urgency, endless scroll or rankings") has to be answered first. A post that disappears in 24 hours, with an unseen ring, is exactly the urgency pattern Q4 warns about. Options: **ADAPT** (keep the short note, drop the ring and any countdown, so there is nothing to miss) or **DEFER**. _Recommended: ADAPT; DEFER if you would rather not publish disappearing content at all._ (An earlier version of this file called Q4 "the moderation review". That was a mistake: it is the attention test.)

Open `p2-05-moments.sql` first if you only read one: it is the only one that changes what a small ministry can publish.
